Ni-Cd vs Ni-MH: the two chemistries explained

Makita's classic pods were originally Ni-Cd (nickel-cadmium). Modern replacements are almost always Ni-MH (nickel-metal-hydride). Same voltage, same pod — but very different behaviour:

FactorNi-Cd (original)Ni-MH (recommended)
Typical capacity1,300–2,000 mAh3,000 mAh (≈50% more)
Memory effectStrong — must fully dischargeNegligible
Self-dischargeLowSlightly higher (modern cells are much improved)
Weight & sizeHeavier for the capacityLighter per amp-hour
EnvironmentalCadmium is toxic, stricter recyclingCadmium-free, easier to recycle
Best forBudget spares, occasional light useDaily trade use, maximum runtime
Bottom line: For the same pod, Ni-MH 3,000 mAh runs roughly 50% longer than a Ni-Cd 2,000 mAh pack — and you can top it up anytime without "memory" penalty. That's why we default every PA12/PA14/PA18 buyer to Ni-MH.

Which chemistry should you buy?

  • Daily trade / serious DIY: Ni-MH 3,000 mAh every time. More runtime, no memory effect, just charge and go.
  • Occasional light use / tight budget: A Ni-Cd 2,000 mAh pack is the cheapest way to get a 14.4V tool (e.g. 6319D) running again.
  • Two-battery strategy: Buy two Ni-MH pods and rotate — one on the tool, one on the charger. Common on 6391D job sites.
  • Upgrading an old Ni-Cd tool: Switch to Ni-MH; just confirm your charger supports Ni-MH (most Makita multi-chemistry chargers do).

Frequently asked questions

Is Ni-MH really worth the extra cost over Ni-Cd?
For almost everyone, yes. You get roughly 50% more runtime per charge and no memory effect, so the cost-per-hour-of-use is lower — and you spend less time at the charger.
Will a 3,000 mAh pack fit where my old 2,000 mAh pack was?
Yes. Capacity doesn't change the pod housing — a 3,000 mAh Ni-MH pod drops into the same handle and simply runs longer.
Why might my 18V pack run fewer minutes than a 12V one?
18V tools draw more current under load. Minutes-per-charge can be lower, but the 18V pack does far more work (torque/speed). Compare by the job done, not just the clock.
Can I mix Ni-Cd and Ni-MH packs on the same tool?
Yes — voltage and pod are what matter, not chemistry. Just use a charger that supports whatever chemistry you're charging at the time.
