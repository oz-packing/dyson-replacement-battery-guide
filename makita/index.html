Why replacing the battery beats replacing the tool

Makita's classic 12V–18V pod-battery tools were built for decades of service — it's the Ni-Cd and Ni-MH packs that wear out. A quality replacement battery typically costs a fraction of even a second-hand tool, keeps perfectly good motors and gearboxes in service, and with modern Ni-MH cells can deliver more runtime than the original pack ever did. This guide is written for Australian owners who want the facts before they buy.

Quick fact: PA12, PA14, 1420 and PA18 are all pod-style packs — they slide into the tool's handle and lock with a click. Swapping takes seconds, no tools required. The skill is in choosing the right chemistry and capacity, and commissioning the new pack properly.

Do you actually need a new battery? (60-second self-check)

Symptom 1: Drill runs strong for 2 minutes, then dies on a "full" charge.
Symptom 2: Battery shows full but tool bogs down under light load.
Symptom 3: Charger light goes green suspiciously fast (minutes, not an hour).
Symptom 4: Pack gets hot while charging, or won't take charge at all.

Ticked two or more? Your cells are at end of life — or suffering classic Ni-Cd memory effect. A full discharge/charge cycle (see the revive guide) is worth one try; if runtime is still poor, replace it. Use the compatibility chart to find your exact pack.

Note: Before blaming the battery, check the charger too — a faulty charger can undercharge a healthy pack. Our troubleshooting table shows how to tell them apart in 5 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a Ni-MH battery in a tool that came with Ni-Cd?
Yes — the pod fit and voltage are identical, and Ni-MH gives more runtime with far less memory effect. Just make sure your charger supports Ni-MH (most Makita multi-chemistry chargers do). Details in the replace & revive guide.
Will a 3,000 mAh pack fit where my old 1,300–2,000 mAh pack was?
Yes. Capacity doesn't change the housing — a 3,000 mAh Ni-MH pod slides into the same handle and simply runs longer. See the runtime comparison.
My Makita 6319D needs a battery — which one?
The 6319D is a 14.4V drill: it takes the PA14 / 1420 family. The 18V 6391D takes the PA18 family. Both are covered in the compatibility chart.
Are aftermarket Makita batteries safe?
Yes, from a reputable Australian supplier with certified cells, over-charge/over-discharge protection and a real warranty. Avoid anonymous cheap packs — see our avoid-fakes guide.
