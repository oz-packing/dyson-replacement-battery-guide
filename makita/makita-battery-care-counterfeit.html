Makita Battery Care & How to Avoid Fakes

A well-cared-for Makita pod battery lasts years in Australian conditions. Beating memory effect (Ni-Cd) for good

Ni-Cd cells "remember" shallow discharge depths and then act full when they're not. If your original 6319D or 6391D pack dies after two minutes, memory effect is the usual suspect.

  • Full cycles, not top-ups on Ni-Cd packs: run flat, charge full, repeat 2–3 times to recondition the cells.
  • Upgrade to Ni-MH (PA12/PA14/PA18 all available as 3,000 mAh Ni-MH). Ni-MH has negligible memory effect, so you can charge whenever — no ritual required.
  • Don't store Ni-Cd flat. A fully discharged Ni-Cd left for months can enter a deep-discharge state the charger won't detect. Store it at least half-charged.
Fast rule: If you're fighting memory effect more than once, the cells are telling you they're worn. A fresh Ni-MH pod ends the battle and outruns the original. See the revive guide.

Storage in Australian heat & sheds

Australian summers are brutal on batteries. Heat is the #1 killer of cell life, so storage matters as much as use.

DoDon't
Store at room temp, out of direct sunLeave packs in a hot ute tray or shed roof space
Keep at ~40–60% charge for long storageStore fully flat or constantly 100% for months
Let a hot pack cool before chargingCharge a pack that's still warm from use

Ute / ute-tray owners: A pod left baking in a closed ute cabin on a 40°C day can hit well over 60°C. Bring batteries inside overnight — your runtime and cell life will thank you.

Daily habits that extend battery life

  • Match charger to chemistry. Use a Makita multi-chemistry charger (or quality aftermarket) that supports Ni-MH for Ni-MH packs.
  • Avoid full discharge on Ni-MH. Unlike Ni-Cd, Ni-MH prefers shallow cycles — charge before it's completely flat.
  • Keep terminals clean. A dab of contact cleaner on the metal contacts keeps the connection solid and the tool running at full power.
  • Rotate two packs. One on the tool, one on the charger — common on 6391D sites and the cheapest productivity upgrade there is.
  • Don't overcharge. Smart chargers stop automatically; trickle chargers left on for days slowly cook the cells.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop Ni-Cd memory effect?
Cycle the pack fully (flat then full charge) 2–3 times to recondition it. Better still, upgrade to Ni-MH, which has negligible memory effect and needs no special routine.
Is it OK to leave Makita batteries in a hot ute?
No. Heat is the biggest threat to cell life. Bring packs inside, out of direct sun and away from shed roof-space heat. Let hot packs cool before charging.
How can I tell if a Makita battery is fake?
Watch for prices far below market, no ABN/warranty, misspelled or fuzzy "Makita" branding, missing CE/RoHS marks, and packs that run hot or die in minutes. Buy from a recognised Australian supplier with a written warranty.
Should I store batteries full or empty?
For long storage, keep them around 40–60% charged in a cool, dry place, and top up every couple of months. Avoid storing fully flat (Ni-Cd can go deep-discharge) or constantly at 100%.
