How to read this compatibility chart

Makita's classic cordless tools use pod-style slide-in batteries. The battery slides into the handle and clicks into place — no tools, no screws. Three facts decide whether a pack fits your tool:

  • Voltage must match. A 12V pack only fits 12V tools, a 14.4V pack only fits 14.4V tools, and an 18V pack only fits 18V tools. Mixing voltages is impossible by design (the pods are physically keyed).
  • Pod family / shape must match. Within a voltage, Makita used a consistent pod shell across that generation. PA14 and 1420 share the same 14.4V pod; PA18 is the 18V pod; PA12 is the 12V pod.
  • Chemistry is your choice. The original pack may have been Ni-Cd; a modern Ni-MH pack of the same voltage and pod shape drops straight in and runs longer. See the runtime comparison.
Pro tip: The fastest way to be 100% sure is to read the part number off the original battery's label (e.g. "PA18", "1420", "192826-5"). Every number below links to the matching replacement at AussieBatt.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 1420 battery the same as the PA14?
Yes. The 1420 is the earlier part number for the same 14.4V pod shell used by the PA14. They are fully interchangeable, so a Ni-MH 1420 or PA14 drops into either's tool.
My drill says 14.4V — will a PA18 fit?
No. Voltage must match. A 14.4V tool (e.g. 6319D) takes PA14/1420; an 18V tool (e.g. 6391D) takes PA18. The pods are physically keyed so they can't be forced.
Do I need the exact part number, or just the voltage?
Within a generation, the pod shape is shared across the voltage family, so matching the voltage and pod style is enough. Reading the part number off the old label removes all doubt — that's what our chart lists.
Can I switch from Ni-Cd to Ni-MH?
Yes, and we recommend it. Same voltage, same pod, more runtime, no memory effect. Just use a charger that supports Ni-MH (most Makita multi-chemistry chargers do). See the replace & revive guide.
