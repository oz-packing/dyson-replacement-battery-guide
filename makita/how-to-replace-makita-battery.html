How to Replace & Revive a Makita Battery

Swapping a Makita pod battery takes seconds — the skill is in commissioning a new Ni-MH pack correctly and knowing when a "dead" battery can actually be revived. This Australian guide covers PA12, PA14, PA18 and 1420, including the 6319D and 6391D drills.

Step-by-step: swapping a Makita pod battery

All four workhorse packs — PA12, PA14, 1420 and PA18 — are pod-style batteries that slide into the tool's handle and lock with a click. No screws, no tools.

  1. Remove the old pack. Press the battery-release latch (usually at the base of the handle) and slide the pod out. On most Makita drills the latch is on the back of the grip.
  2. Check the voltage. Confirm the new pack matches: 12V (PA12), 14.4V (PA14 / 1420) or 18V (PA18). The pods are physically keyed so a wrong-voltage pack simply won't seat.
  3. Seat the new pod. Slide it up into the handle until it clicks and the release latch springs back. Give it a gentle tug to confirm it's locked.
  4. Charge before first heavy use. Even a fresh pack should get a full charge on a compatible charger first — see commissioning below.
  5. Test. Run the tool briefly. A new Ni-MH pack should spin the 6319D or 6391D up to full speed without bogging.
Good to know: A higher-capacity pod (e.g. 3,000 mAh Ni-MH) uses the same housing as your old 1,300–2,000 mAh pack — it just runs longer. No fitment change.

Commissioning a new Ni-MH pack (do this right)

A brand-new Ni-MH battery isn't at its best straight out of the box. A simple 2–3 cycle "formation" routine unlocks its full capacity and longest life:

  • Cycle 1: Charge fully → use until the tool noticeably slows → recharge fully.
  • Cycle 2 & 3: Repeat. By the third full charge/discharge the cells settle to their rated capacity.
  • Charger check: Use a Makita multi-chemistry charger (or a quality aftermarket one) that supports Ni-MH. Ni-Cd-only trickle chargers won't "finish" a Ni-MH pack correctly.
  • Don't over-think it: Unlike the old Ni-Cd packs, Ni-MH has no meaningful memory effect — you can top up whenever you like without harming capacity.
Hot battery? If the pack is warm from use, let it cool to room temperature before charging. Charging a hot pack stresses the cells and shortens life — important in the Australian summer.

Can a "dead" battery be revived? (before you spend a cent)

Before buying a replacement, two cheap fixes are worth trying — especially on older Ni-Cd packs.

1. Cure Ni-Cd memory effect

Ni-Cd cells "remember" a shallow discharge and act full when they're not. Fix it with a deep discharge + full recharge: run the tool (or a lamp/bulb across the terminals) until it's truly flat, then give it a long, uninterrupted charge. Repeat 2–3 times. Many packs that "died at 2 minutes" come back to life.

2. Wake a deeply discharged pack

If a pack has sat flat for months, the charger may refuse it (it can't detect a voltage to charge). Try a bump charge: briefly touch the terminals to a healthy same-voltage pack or a low-current source for a few seconds to raise the voltage above the charger's detection threshold, then charge normally. If it won't take a charge after 2 attempts, the cells are worn — replace it.

When reviving isn't worth it: If the pack is physically swollen, leaks, or gets hot while charging, stop — that's end-of-life. A fresh Ni-MH replacement is cheaper than the time spent fighting dead cells, and it'll outrun the original.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need any tools to swap a Makita pod battery?
No. Pod batteries slide into the handle and lock with a latch — a tool-free swap that takes seconds. Just match the voltage.
My old battery is Ni-Cd but the replacement is Ni-MH — will it work?
Yes. Same voltage, same pod, more runtime, no memory effect. Use a charger that supports Ni-MH (most Makita multi-chemistry chargers do).
Can I really revive a dead Ni-Cd pack?
Often yes, if it's memory effect or deep discharge rather than worn-out cells. A few deep discharge/recharge cycles or a bump charge can bring it back. If it's swollen or won't hold a charge after two tries, replace it.
Why won't my charger recognise a flat pack?
Chargers need a minimum voltage to detect a pack. A long-flat battery may sit below that threshold. A brief bump charge to wake it usually solves it; otherwise the cells are spent.
